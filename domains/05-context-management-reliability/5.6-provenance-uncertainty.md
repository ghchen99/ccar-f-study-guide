# 5.6 Preserve information provenance and handle uncertainty in multi-source synthesis

## At a glance

- How source attribution is lost during summarization steps when findings are compressed without preserving claim-source mappings.
- The importance of structured claim-source mappings that the synthesis agent must preserve and merge when combining findings.
- How to handle conflicting statistics from credible sources: annotating conflicts with source attribution rather than arbitrarily selecting one value.
- Temporal data: requiring publication/collection dates in structured outputs to prevent temporal differences from being misinterpreted as contradictions.

## Example

### Subagent output: claim-source mappings with dates

```python
from typing import TypedDict


class Finding(TypedDict):
    claim: str
    evidence_excerpt: str          # lets a reader check the claim without refetching the source
    source_url: str | None
    document_name: str | None      # either URL or document name identifies the source; both optional for that reason
    page: int | None
    published: str                 # ISO date of publication / data collection; separates change over time from contradiction
    methodology: str | None        # e.g. "survey, n=2,000"; explains why credible sources can differ
```

The synthesis agent must preserve and merge these mappings. If attribution is dropped during summarisation, claims become unsourceable.

### Conflicting values: annotate, do not pick

```json
{
  "claim": "Share of studios using generative tools",
  "values": [
    {"value": "62%", "source": "https://a.example/report", "published": "2025-06-01", "methodology": "survey, n=800"},
    {"value": "41%", "source": "https://b.example/study",  "published": "2024-02-15", "methodology": "survey, n=2,000"}
  ],
  "conflict": true,
  "note": "Different years and sample sizes; may reflect growth, not disagreement"
}
```

Include both values in document analysis and let the coordinator decide how to reconcile before synthesis. Dates matter: a 2024 figure and a 2025 figure are a time difference, not a contradiction.

### Report structure that separates established from contested

```markdown
## Well-established findings
- AI image tools are widely used in concept art (4 independent sources: [1][2][3][4])

## Contested findings
- Adoption rate: 41% (Study B, 2024) vs 62% (Report A, 2025). Different dates and samples; see sources.

## Coverage gaps
- Music licensing: no source available (search timed out)
```

### Render each content type appropriately

| Content | Format |
| - | - |
| Financial figures, comparisons | Table |
| News and context | Prose |
| Technical findings | Structured list |

Do not flatten everything into one uniform bullet format.

### Synthesis instruction

```text
For every claim keep its source_url/document_name and published date. When two credible
sources give different figures, report both with attribution and the context (date,
methodology). Do not choose one. Put widely corroborated findings and disputed findings
in separate sections.
```

## Why this matters

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The principle**

Every claim carries its provenance (source, excerpt, date, methodology) through each stage of a pipeline, and uncertainty is represented explicitly rather than resolved silently. Subagents emit structured claim-source mappings; synthesis preserves and merges them; conflicts between credible sources are reported side by side with attribution; and the report separates what is well established from what is contested or missing. The principle: an agent that lacks the authority or evidence to decide between sources should surface the disagreement to the coordinator or reader, not hide it.

**The flow**

- `Finding` bundles claim, excerpt, locator (URL or document name and page), date and methodology. Each field answers a later question: Where did this come from? Can I verify it? How old is it? How was it measured? Compressing findings into prose in an intermediate step drops these, and a claim without a source cannot be checked or cited.
- `published` is required because dates turn apparent contradictions into trends. A 2024 figure of 41% and a 2025 figure of 62% may describe growth, not disagreement. Without dates the synthesis would flag a conflict that does not exist or, worse, pick one.
- `methodology` explains legitimate differences (sample size, population, definition) between sources that are each credible.
- The conflict record keeps both values with their own source, date and methodology and sets `conflict: true` with a note. The decision about reconciliation is left to the coordinator or reader, who has context the subagent lacks. Averaging would manufacture a number that no source reported; arbitrarily choosing hides a real disagreement.
- Document analysis completes with conflicts annotated, rather than stopping at the first disagreement, so the rest of the work is not blocked and the coordinator receives everything needed to decide.
- The three-section report (well established, contested, coverage gaps) shows reliability at a glance. Corroborated claims with several independent sources are separated from single-source or disputed ones, and a gap from an unavailable source is stated (linking back to 5.3).
- The format table (tables for financial data, prose for news, lists for technical findings) preserves each content type's structure; flattening everything into bullets can destroy relationships such as comparisons between figures.
- The synthesis instruction repeats the rules where the model will act on them: keep source and date, report both values, do not choose, separate sections.

## Trade-offs

- **Rich provenance vs token cost:** carrying excerpts, dates and methodology for every claim uses context and complicates schemas. It is warranted whenever outputs are cited, audited or acted on; for throwaway summaries a lighter form may do.
- **Surface conflict vs resolve it:** surfacing is honest and shifts work to the reader; resolving gives a cleaner answer but only when a justified rule exists (for example, prefer the more recent or higher-quality source, stated explicitly and recorded). An implicit pick is never justified.
- **Resolve at coordinator vs at synthesis:** the coordinator has the broader view and can request more evidence; a synthesis agent working on a fixed input cannot.
- **Excerpt length:** short excerpts keep context small; longer excerpts support verification. Include a pointer so the full source can be fetched.
- **Consistent format vs type-appropriate format:** a uniform format is simple to produce; type-appropriate rendering is easier to read and keeps structure.
- **Corroboration counting:** counting independent sources strengthens "well-established" labels but needs care that sources are truly independent.

> [!WARNING]
> **Exam traps**

- "Average the two statistics" or "use the more recent one without saying so": inventing or silently choosing a value. Rule: annotate the conflict with source attribution and let the coordinator or reader decide.
- "Pick the figure from the most authoritative-looking source": arbitrary selection hides the disagreement. Rule: do not arbitrarily choose between conflicting credible sources.
- "Summarise findings into a short paragraph before synthesis to save space": attribution is lost. Rule: pass structured claim-source mappings, not prose.
- "Treat different values as contradictory": the values may come from different dates. Rule: require publication or collection dates so time differences are not misread as conflicts.
- "Drop the claim when sources disagree": loses information. Rule: keep both with context.
- "Format every section as bullets for consistency": distorts content types. Rule: render financial data as tables, news as prose and technical findings as lists.

## Quick check

- Two reputable reports give 41% and 62% for the same adoption statistic. What should the synthesis do? Report both values with their sources, dates and methodologies, mark the claim as contested, and note that the difference may reflect timing or sample, rather than choosing or averaging.
- A final report contains claims that cannot be traced to a source. What went wrong upstream? Attribution was dropped during summarisation; subagents must output claim-source mappings that synthesis preserves and merges.
- A statistic changed between 2023 and 2025 in two sources. Is this a conflict? Not necessarily; with dates present it can be reported as a change over time, which is why publication dates are required in structured output.
